# Pricing Research Methods

Use one method for the decision it can answer. Record the offer description, respondent or account segment, recruitment source, dates, question order, missing responses, and analysis choices. Stated answers are evidence about stated preferences under that prompt; they do not establish realized demand, retention, margin, or a universal price.

## Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter

Ask the same product description and cadence for four price perceptions: a price so low it raises quality concerns, a bargain, becoming expensive while still considered, and so expensive it would not be considered. Plot the cumulative distributions and label the conventional intersections: too-cheap with expensive (point of marginal cheapness), too-expensive with cheap (point of marginal expensiveness), too-cheap with too-expensive (often called the optimal price point), and expensive with cheap (indifference price point).

The resulting perceived acceptable range describes this sample's answers to those questions. It is not a demand curve, a behavioral purchase forecast, or a profit-maximizing price. Compare segments only when their offer context and sampling permit it; investigate large nonresponse or implausible answers rather than silently normalizing them.

## Gabor-Granger

Ask comparable respondents whether they would buy a specified offer at a specified price. Randomize or otherwise balance price exposure and retain the full offer, currency, cadence, alternatives, and purchase-intent wording. The price-by-stated-intent relationship can compare candidate points under the survey conditions. It is not the same as Van Westendorp: it asks purchase intent at explicit prices rather than perceived price boundaries. It remains vulnerable to hypothetical and sampling bias, so do not represent it as observed demand.

## MaxDiff

Show balanced subsets of capabilities or package elements and ask which is most and least important in each set. Analyze relative importance within the defined respondent population. MaxDiff helps prioritize what to investigate in packaging; it does not measure willingness to pay, quantify price sensitivity, or assign items to tiers automatically. Check segment-level results before using pooled ranks. A capability can be broadly important, expected in every package, operationally expensive, or irrelevant to a particular buyer, producing different packaging choices.

## Behavioral learning

When authorized and safe, a controlled offer comparison with a predefined eligible population and outcome can complement stated-preference research. Preserve existing contractual commitments and measure the full decision, including conversion, revenue realization, support burden, retention window, and margin where relevant. A short result may not answer renewal or long-term demand. Document the owner, guardrails, stop/revise rule, and limits; no study itself authorizes a billing change.
