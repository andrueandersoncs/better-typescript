# Pricing

Read the [shared marketing context](../context.md) before working. Reuse its supplied/project artifacts and the existing product record; do not create a parallel pricing or product record. This workflow prepares a pricing decision or experiment. It cannot send a quote, publish a price, change billing, alter entitlements, buy a tool, or commit a customer.

## Frame the decision

Separate the three variables before recommending any change:

- **Packaging**: which capabilities, limits, service levels, or terms are offered together.
- **Value metric**: the unit that changes as customer value changes, such as seats, usage, transactions, or a flat commitment.
- **Price point**: the amount and cadence charged for a defined package and metric.

Identify the actual buyer, user, approver, and payer where they differ. Establish the customer outcome and credible value proxy; alternative and competitor offers; route to market; cost-to-serve, margin, and capacity constraints; current revenue and conversion evidence; and existing commitments such as contracts, notices, discounts, grandfathering, regulated terms, or operational limits. Missing inputs remain unknown rather than becoming assumed ratios, price thresholds, or a generic tier model.

## Evaluate evidence and options

Trace each finding to its source, date, population, method, and limitations. Separate observed behavior and contractual facts from inferences and proposals. Segment by decision context when evidence supports it; do not pool buyer types, deal sizes, regions, or uses into one ranking and call the result universal. Customer statements about price reveal context and perceived tradeoffs, not automatically demand or margin.

Develop options that vary one pricing axis at a time where practical. For each, state the customer it serves, intended value exchange, operational and margin implications, migration impact, evidence for and against it, assumptions, and what result would revise or stop the proposal. A value metric should have an intelligible connection to customer value, be measurable and auditable, and avoid charging unexpectedly for normal use. Packaging must make the included outcome and limits legible; feature ranks alone do not determine tiers.

Use [pricing research methods](references/research-methods.md) when choosing a study. Match the method to its question rather than treating stated willingness to pay as purchase behavior. Do not present a perceived acceptable range as a demand curve or profit optimum, claim that an increase preserves demand, infer tier boundaries automatically from pooled ranks, or promise a price outcome from a small sample.

## Recommend reversible learning

When evidence is inadequate, propose the smallest lawful, authorized, reversible learning step. Define the eligible population, current offer, candidate offer, measurement rule, exposure period, guardrails, decision owner, and response if results differ. Keep existing-customer commitments explicit; their treatment needs an approved policy, not an assumption. Use [predictive planning](../../../predictive-planning/SKILL.md) for consequential forecasts and response rules.

Return a concise decision brief: decision and scope; buyer and constraints; current facts and evidence quality; options and tradeoffs; recommended test or decision; assumptions, risks, and owner. Send durable product behavior, entitlements, and accepted change intent to [product management](../../../product-management/SKILL.md). Pricing-page wording belongs to copywriting; billing implementation belongs to its established owner.
