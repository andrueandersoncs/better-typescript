# Customer Research

Read the [shared marketing context](../context.md) before working. Reuse the supplied and project artifacts it identifies, including the existing product record; do not create a shadow marketing record. This workflow owns evidence collection and synthesis. [Product management](../../../product-management/SKILL.md) owns durable product claims and change intent.

## Choose evidence mode

Start with the decision, target segment, and evidence already available. Ask only for the missing detail that changes the next step.

1. **Existing assets**: analyze supplied transcripts, sales or support conversations, survey exports, reviews, win/loss notes, and behavioral data when they can answer the decision.
2. **Public signal**: when first-party assets are absent or incomplete, collect relevant public posts, reviews, forums, job postings, and competitor reviews. Treat them as signals from the speakers represented, not from the market as a whole.
3. **Primary research**: plan interviews or a survey when the decision requires direct accounts, causal context, or a defined segment that the other modes cannot represent.

Modes may be combined. Mine available evidence before designing primary questions so the research tests real alternatives and uses the audience's language. Do not browse, contact people, or publish anything unless the request permits it.

## Build an auditable evidence set

For every usable item, retain source or artifact identifier, publication or interview date, collection method, relevant role/account/use-case context, and exact quote or observed behavior. Keep enough surrounding context to prevent a quote from changing meaning. Note selection limits: support cases overrepresent trouble; public reviewers and communities self-select; a single account, campaign, thread, author, repost, or syndicated review is one correlated source, not several independent observations.

Tag each datum with segment membership only when observed or supplied. State denominators precisely: for example, “mentioned in 7 of 11 reviewed documents from 4 independent accounts,” never a population rate unless the sampling supports one. Preserve disconfirming evidence and contradictions; explain plausible scope, timing, or method differences without resolving them by assertion.

Separate the synthesis:

- **Fact**: directly observed behavior or a traceable verbatim statement.
- **Inference**: a bounded interpretation, with its supporting items and alternative explanations.
- **Hypothesis**: a proposed explanation, segment, message, or product change to test.

Do not turn a few sources, repeated claims, or a proxy source into high confidence. Do not invent a persona. A segment description is provisional until evidence consistently identifies its shared context, job, and decision constraints.

## Primary research

State the purpose transparently, obtain appropriate consent, and describe how notes or recordings will be used. Recruit against the requested decision segment rather than whoever is easiest to reach; record who declined or is missing when that could bias conclusions. Ask about observed past behavior: “Tell me about the last time you…,” “What happened before you looked for an alternative?,” “What did you try, decide, and pay for?,” and “What changed afterward?” Follow the participant's terms. Avoid pitches, leading assumptions, hypothetical preference questions standing in for behavior, and questions that imply the desired answer.

## Deliver the requested result

For a synthesis, provide the decision, evidence scope and limitations, segment-specific themes, representative dated quotes, contradictions, and clearly labeled facts, inferences, and hypotheses. Link relevant findings to the existing product record rather than copying mutable product truth into a marketing artifact. For a research request, return a bounded plan: decision, target segment and recruiting frame, method, evidence fields, questions, analysis rule, and the condition that would change the decision. Use [predictive planning](../../../predictive-planning/SKILL.md) only when the request needs a forecast or response policy.
