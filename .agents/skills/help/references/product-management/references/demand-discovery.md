# Demand discovery

Use when deciding whether to build, narrow, or expand a product. Start with the decision and existing evidence, not a mandatory interview. Keep findings in the existing product record.

## Select the unresolved question

| Available product evidence | Questions that can change the decision |
| --- | --- |
| No product or users yet | Who has experienced this problem? What happened the last time? What do they do instead, and what does that cost them? |
| People use a prototype or product | Which useful result brings them back? What is the smallest complete workflow that delivers it? What happened when someone tried it without guidance? |
| Paying or otherwise committed users | What do they repeatedly rely on, renew, or expand? What would make them switch away? Which proposed expansion follows observed demand rather than enthusiasm? |
| Internal tool or infrastructure | Whose workflow changes, what time or failure cost is observed, and what small result would justify continued use or sponsor commitment? |

Use the applicable row as a starting point, not a maturity score or required sequence. Reuse answers already supplied. Ask only for missing information that could change the next action; respect a request to proceed without more questions and retain the uncertainty.

## Separate evidence from the pitch

- Identify a real actor, their task, the triggering situation, and the consequence. Use an existing participant identifier rather than demanding personal names.
- Distinguish expressed interest, trial, repeated use, payment, and dependence. A waitlist is evidence of interest, not proof of sustained demand; payment is not the only meaningful commitment for internal, public, or free products.
- Describe the current workaround and its observed cost, including doing nothing. Absence of a workaround leaves the reason unresolved; it does not prove absence of pain.
- Separate an assisted demonstration from unassisted use. Record where the user hesitated, recovered, abandoned, or used the product unexpectedly. A survey or founder interpretation is not direct observation.
- Compare the smallest useful workflow with the proposed scope. Do not turn every question into a platform redesign or assume the smallest feature is useful by itself.

For additional evidence collection or interview design, use the existing [customer-research workflow](../../marketing/references/customer-research/SKILL.md). It owns research; this reference uses the results to inform the product decision.

## Decide and learn

Record the recommendation, supporting and contrary evidence, unresolved assumption, and smallest authorized action that could change the decision. State the observation that would support continuing, narrowing, or stopping before running that action. Do not invent thresholds, market confidence, or urgency.

A sufficient next action might be observing the target task without coaching or testing whether a specific user adopts a bounded workflow. Proposing research does not authorize contacting people, collecting personal data, charging customers, or building the product. Use [predictive-planning](../../predictive-planning/SKILL.md) if the request needs a forecast or response policy.
